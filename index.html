    What the data says

    What do these terms mean?

    BOD — Biochemical Oxygen Demand
    The oxygen that microbes need to break down the organic pollution in water. Higher BOD means more polluted water. The bathing standard is 3 mg/l or less.
    DO — Dissolved Oxygen
    The oxygen dissolved in the water — the oxygen fish breathe. Higher is better; below about 5 mg/l, fish struggle. The bathing standard is 5 mg/l or more.
    STP — Sewage Treatment Plant
    A plant that treats sewage before it is released into the river. Its size is given in MLD (million litres per day). Its outlet water is judged against NGT (2019) and MoEFCC (2017) norms.
    Water-quality station
    A monitoring point, usually run by the CPCB, where river water is sampled regularly for BOD, DO, pH and faecal coliform.
    MLD — million litres per day
    The unit for flows: how much water a drain carries, or how much sewage a plant can treat, each day.
    TPD — tonnes per day
    The unit for pollution load: how many tonnes of BOD a drain adds to the river each day.
    Tapped / untapped drain
    A tapped drain is intercepted — its sewage is diverted into the sewer network and (in theory) to a treatment plant. An untapped drain flows straight into the river.
    NGT norms
    The discharge limits set by the National Green Tribunal in 2019 (metro tier: BOD 10 mg/l). The binding notified rule is the looser MoEFCC 2017 standard.

    How Indian norms compare with the world

    India’s NGT limits are among the strictest on paper for conventional pollutants — BOD 10 beats the EU’s 25 and the US’s 30 — but the notified MoEFCC 2017 rule is far looser, and the EU recast leads on nutrients and micropollutants.

    NGT figures are the metro tier of a tiered table (Class I towns: BOD 20), not a notified uniform rule. NH₄-N 5 is a 2015 draft target, not an operative limit — the operative nutrient limit is total nitrogen 10. No per-plant total nitrogen value is published anywhere. Lower is stricter.           Watch live (when on)

          There is no open, 24/7, embeddable riverfront webcam network for these rivers. What exists is scheduled aarti / darshan streams — link-outs only, on at aarti hours, offline in between.           Where no live camera was found

            Live data instead of video

            For the river itself, live numbers beat a camera. CPCB runs about 40 Real-Time Water Quality Monitoring Stations on the Ganga and its tributaries, hourly — the dashboard load was not verified live in this session, and the 40-station figure rests on a Parliament answer of 16 Mar 2026. NMCG's GangaPulse shows STP performance from OCEMS sensors — a treatment layer, not a river-health layer.
